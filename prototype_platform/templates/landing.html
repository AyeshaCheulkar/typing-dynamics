Typing & Mind-space Researcher login

Your typing has a rhythm. Feelings may change it.

When you pause, rush, or go back to fix a word, your keyboard quietly records how your mind is moving. This study looks at how remembering a happy or a sad moment shapes that rhythm: your focus, your hesitation, and the steadiness of your mind-space.

Begin About five minutes. No questionnaires.

  1. Settle in

    A short, neutral warm-up so we learn your own usual typing rhythm.

  2. Write freely

    Choose a happy or a sad moment and write about whatever comes to mind. There is no right way.

  3. See your pattern

    Get a gentle report on your rhythm, focus and the tone of your words.

We read how you type, not who you are

The report describes patterns in your typing and the emotional tone of your text. It is a research prototype, and it is not a diagnosis or a measure of your mental health.

Choose a moment you are comfortable recalling. You can stop at any time by closing the page.

Pauses and hesitation
How often, and for how long, you stop to think.
Rhythm steadiness
How even the timing is from one keystroke to the next.
Self-correction
How much you go back, delete and rephrase.
Tone of your words
Whether what you wrote reads as positive, negative or mixed.
Research prototype. Your keystroke timing and text are stored under your participant ID for this study.
