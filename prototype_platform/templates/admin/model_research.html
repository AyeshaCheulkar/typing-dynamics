Typing skill and keyboard

Both are asked before writing and shown on the report, because they move the baseline of every timing measure.

Evidence (Inputlog copy-task corpus, 491 typists)

  • Median key interval: 188 ms for the slowest third, 148 ms for the middle, 124 ms for the fastest third.
  • Laptop typists (242) had shorter intervals than desktop typists (238): 142 vs 164 ms mean, p < 0.001. This is confounded with who uses which device, so it justifies adjusting for device, not a causal claim.
  • Faster typists have slightly steadier rhythm (ρ = −0.12, p = 0.007).

How it is used

  • Key speed is compared with writers after a laptop vs desktop adjustment taken from the corpus.
  • The skill answer words the report (a person who looks at the keys is told the pauses partly reflect key search).
  • On the Emotion & state page, the questionnaire-vs-typing correlations are also shown adjusted for skill and keyboard.
  • The readings themselves are not rescaled: the pilot and EmoSurv data have no skill or device labels to base that on.
