What we test

Each state is measured twice, independently: once by a published questionnaire the participant answers, and once by a typing reading computed only from keystrokes. The hypothesis is that the two move together.

StateParticipant's answer (ground truth)Typing readingExpected link
Emotional intensitySelf-Assessment Manikinmean(felt strongly, arousal) Emotional-processing loadmean(hesitation, self-correction)More deleting and pausing with stronger emotion (H4)
Cognitive loadRaw NASA-TLXmean(mental demand, effort, frustration) Mind-space loadmean(hesitation, self-correction, rhythm irregularity)More strained typing with heavier load (H2)
FlowFlow Short Scalemean of fluent, concentrated, absorbed, lost track of time Focus index100 − mean(time paused, rhythm irregularity, long pauses)More continuous, even typing with more flow (H3)

Typing readings are equal-weight averages of percentile ranks against a reference sample, so each reads 0–100 and no weights are fitted. Questionnaires are shortened to five points. Full formulas: research/docs/FORMULAS.md.

How to read this

What the evidence allows

  • Typing differs on average between moods and between emotional and neutral writing.
  • Rhythm steadiness is the most consistent signal.
  • These are group-level patterns from public data, with our own confirmation still to come.

What it does not allow

  • Telling a happy memory from a sad one by typing alone.
  • Saying how any one person feels, or diagnosing anything.
  • Treating the typing readings as validated measures of load, flow or intensity before H2–H4 are supported.

Prior work: Epp, Lippold & Mandryk (2011); Vizer, Zhou & Sears (2009); BiAffect studies of typing and mood; the EmoSurv dataset (Maalej & Kallel); VADER (Hutto & Gilbert, 2014); first-person pronouns in emotional writing (Rude, Gortner & Pennebaker, 2004).
